Abstract

We learn explicit, class-conditioned spatial priors for object placement in natural scenes by distilling placement knowledge from text-conditioned diffusion models. Prior methods rely on sparse manual annotations or on inpainting object-removal pipelines, whose artifacts promote shortcut learning. To address these limitations, we propose a scalable pipeline that evaluates dense candidate placements by inserting objects into clean backgrounds. This produces HiddenObjects, a dataset of 27M bounding-box annotations (2M positives) across 27k diverse scenes and object categories. Human evaluators prefer our image editing composites over those guided by sparse annotations in 65.6% of comparisons. We distill the priors into a lightweight placement model that achieves the best accuracy among dedicated baselines and zero-shot vision-language models.

Dataset

We construct HiddenObjects by pairing 27k background images from 126 scene categories of Places365 with 50 COCO foreground categories across 10 macro-classes (food, furniture, animal, vehicle, …). Each scene–category pair is annotated with 1,004 ranked bounding box proposals, yielding 27M placement annotations in total.

Where and How Big Are the Objects?

Method

① Inpainter

Qwen-Image + ControlNet synthesises class-consistent object insertions at each bounding box in a sliding-window grid. ControlNet conditioning enforces scene geometry and refuses physically impossible placements.

② Verifier

Grounded-SAM-2 detects whether the target object was successfully inpainted in the proposed region. Low-confidence detections, wrong categories, and object replacements are discarded.

③ Ranker

ImageReward assigns a human-preference score to each verified insertion. The ranked scores define the final spatial prior distribution over all valid locations.

Example spatial prior creation. Our pipeline performs an exhaustive sliding-window search on a kitchen scene for pizza insertions.

Faster Inpainting with Early Exit

Most candidate boxes end up with a missing or wrong object. The L1 gap between the conditional and unconditional noise predictions after a single denoising step already flags faulty boxes with 81% recall. We skip those boxes, leaving only 30% of proposals for full inpainting: a 2.4× speedup without degrading the prior.

Do Humans Agree with Our Labels?

We audit the pipeline labels on 50 random HiddenObjects test scenes. Annotators see a 3×3 grid with the top-3 verifier-accepted boxes and six verifier-rejected boxes, and pick the three most plausible ones for the target class: 505 responses from 10 annotators, 450 distinct boxes, 4,545 box-level judgments.

Model Distillation

We distill the priors into a lightweight transformer that predicts ranked boxes from a background image and an object class. It runs at 3.77 ms/image with 188.9 MB of GPU memory on an H100, versus 14.49 min and 65.9 GB for the full pipeline: 230,000× faster and 300× less memory.
